# Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY)

## Overview
PMJDY is a national financial inclusion scheme aimed at ensuring access to banking services for every household in India. It provides for basic savings bank accounts with no minimum balance requirement, along with insurance and overdraft facilities.

## Eligibility Criteria
- Any Indian citizen without an existing bank account can open a PMJDY account.
- No minimum age restriction for account opening (minors can have accounts operated by a guardian).
- No income criteria; the scheme is open to all, but primarily targets unbanked and underbanked individuals.
- Must not already hold a Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account (BSBDA) at the same bank (to avoid duplication).

## Documents Required
- Aadhaar card (preferred, as it enables simplified KYC and direct benefit transfer linkage).
- If Aadhaar is not available: any officially valid document (OVD) such as Voter ID, Driving License, Passport, or NREGA job card.
- For those without any of the above (Small Account category): a self-attested photograph and signature/thumb impression in the presence of a bank official; such accounts have transaction and balance limits and require regular KYC updates.
- Passport-size photograph.

## How to Apply
1. Visit any bank branch (public or private sector bank participating in PMJDY) or Bank Mitra/Business Correspondent outlet.
2. Fill out the PMJDY account opening form with personal and Aadhaar details.
3. Complete biometric or OTP-based e-KYC verification.
4. Account is activated immediately upon successful verification, and a RuPay debit card is issued.

Online pre-registration is not typically available; account opening requires an in-person visit for KYC and biometric verification.

## Benefits
- Zero-balance savings account (no minimum balance penalty).
- Free RuPay debit card with accidental insurance cover (coverage amount periodically revised, commonly around Rs. 1-2 lakh for new accounts opened after a certain date).
- Overdraft facility of up to Rs. 10,000 for eligible account holders (subject to satisfactory account operation for 6 months).
- Life insurance cover under linked schemes (subject to eligibility and enrollment, e.g. PMJJBY) for eligible accounts.
- Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) of government subsidies and welfare payments directly into the account.
- Access to pension and insurance products (e.g., Atal Pension Yojana, PM Suraksha Bima Yojana) can be linked to this account.

## Processing Time
Account opening is typically instant to same-day if all documents (especially Aadhaar) are in order and e-KYC is successful. RuPay debit card delivery by post may take 1-2 weeks.

## Common Errors to Avoid
- Opening a duplicate account at the same or different bank, which can cause issues with DBT and benefit linkage.
- Not activating/using the RuPay card periodically - prolonged inactivity may lead to insurance cover lapsing, in some bank policies.
- Incomplete or mismatched Aadhaar-bank linkage, which can delay DBT credits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is there any charge for opening a Jan Dhan account?**
A: No, account opening under PMJDY is completely free of cost.

**Q: Can I convert my existing regular savings account into a Jan Dhan account?**
A: Yes, many banks allow conversion of an existing basic savings account into a PMJDY account; you should check with your specific bank branch.

**Q: What is the overdraft facility and who is eligible?**
A: The overdraft facility allows eligible account holders to withdraw up to Rs. 10,000 beyond their account balance; eligibility typically requires satisfactory operation of the account for at least 6 months, as assessed by the bank.

**Q: Does the insurance cover apply automatically?**
A: The accidental insurance cover on the RuPay card is typically automatic upon card issuance and usage, but the exact terms, conditions, and coverage amount should be confirmed with the issuing bank as they can be revised periodically.

**Q: Can a minor open a Jan Dhan account?**
A: Yes, minors above 10 years can operate the account themselves in some banks, while younger minors' accounts are operated by a natural guardian.
